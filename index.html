We don’t tell you how to feel. We help you find ideas that change how you see it.

Second Look listens to your situation, works out the question underneath it, and gives you a short set of carefully chosen passages from real writers, including at least one that disagrees with you. You read, react, and notice what shifts.

How it works

A clear, guided process for making sense of what you’re feeling.

1. Tell us what’s happening. In your own words, as much or as little as you like.

2. We find the question underneath. “Do I miss her, or the idea of us?” “Is this drift, or is something wrong?” You choose the one that feels closest.

3. Get your deck. Five short cards, in a deliberate order, each credited and linked to its writer.

4. React. Tap That’s me, That stung, or Not me on each card. Your reactions shape what you see next.

5. Say what you think now. One sentence. Over the next days, one more card arrives each day.

Note: We’re starting with breakups and relationship doubt, and adding more situations later.

6. What’s inside a deck

Each deck mixes five kinds of card.

Name it:

Puts words to what you’re feeling.

Reframe:

A different way to look at the same facts.

The other side:

The strongest view that disagrees with you.

A story:

How someone else lived through something similar.

A small thing to try:

One low-pressure experiment, never an order.

Sample deck A: After a breakup

The question you’d pick: Do I miss them, or the life I had with them?

Name it. Missing someone is rarely only about them. You’re also missing the plans, the private jokes, and the ordinary Tuesdays you thought were still coming. That’s why this can feel bigger than the relationship looked from outside.

Reframe. Right after an ending, the mind wants a clean story: it was perfect, or it was a mistake. The truer version usually holds both. You don’t have to pick one yet.

The other side. You may be sure you want them back. Before you act on that, ask a second question: do I miss them, or do I miss not feeling like this? The answers can lead to different places, and only you can tell which one is yours.

A story. (Illustrative.) Emma kept their last conversation open for weeks, rereading it for the line where things went wrong. One evening she noticed she wasn’t reading to understand them anymore. She was reading to argue with it. She didn’t know yet what that meant. But for the first time in weeks, she was asking a question about herself instead of about them.

A small thing to try. Only if it feels right: write down one thing you learned about yourself in this relationship that you’d keep, whatever happens next. It isn’t a verdict on the relationship. It’s something that belongs to you.

Sample deck B: Not sure about us

The question you’d pick: Is this a rough patch, or is something actually wrong?

Name it. Feeling distant from someone you care about is more common than people admit, and from the inside it’s hard to tell whether it’s a season or a sign. Not knowing yet isn’t a failure. It’s an honest place to be.

Reframe. “Are we growing apart?” sounds like one question, but it hides two. Have we changed in different directions? Or have we stopped showing each other what’s changing? The first is about fit. The second is about contact. They’re very different situations to be in.

The other side. Maybe you’re not overthinking. Lasting unease can be information, and talking yourself out of it can be a way of avoiding it. Or maybe this is ordinary wear that feels louder at 2 a.m. than it does at noon. Both are possible, and telling them apart can take more than one conversation.

A story. (Illustrative.) Jake and his partner stopped arguing around the time they stopped talking. He had read the quiet as peace. One night he asked what was on their mind during those silent dinners. They said, “I was wondering if you still wanted to know.” Nothing was settled that night. But it was the first conversation in a long while where neither of them was pretending.

A small thing to try. Optional: privately finish two sentences. “I miss us when…” and “I feel far from us when…” You’re not looking for an answer. You’re noticing which one comes more easily.

Why it’s different

Not a chatbot friend.

No endless chat, no pretending to be human. A short deck with a beginning and an end.

Not a feed.

Nothing to scroll forever. Cards come in an order, with a reason.

Not therapy or advice.

We offer perspectives to think with. What you do with them is yours to decide.

Always the other side.

Every deck includes a view that disagrees with you, because agreeing with you isn’t the same as helping you.

Real writers, always credited.

Every excerpt links to the original.

Made for moments like these

Breakups · Not sure about a relationship · A decision that won’t settle · A career crossroads · Feeling stuck

Starting with relationships. The rest are coming next.
